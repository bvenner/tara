# Jou, Casas-Vázquez & Lebon — Extended Irreversible Thermodynamics; Ruggeri & Sugiyama (2015)

**Citation:** Jou, D., Casas-Vázquez, J., & Lebon, G. (2010). *Extended Irreversible Thermodynamics* (4th ed.). Dordrecht: Springer. Ruggeri, T., & Sugiyama, M. (2015). *Rational Extended Thermodynamics beyond the Monatomic Gas*. Cham: Springer. DOI: 10.1007/978-3-319-13341-6. Comparison survey: Jou, D. (2020), "Relationships between rational extended thermodynamics and extended irreversible thermodynamics," *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A*, 378(2170), 20190172. DOI: 10.1098/rsta.2019.0172.

**Core content:** EIT is the phenomenological sibling of RET: same move (fluxes as independent state variables with evolution equations) but built on classical irreversible thermodynamics rather than the rational balance-law + entropy-principle methodology. EIT is more flexible (wider range of systems: superfluids, polymers, nanosystems) but less restrictive; RET is more constrained (balance-law form, symmetric hyperbolicity, Galilean invariance) but extracts deeper theorems. Ruggeri–Sugiyama extends RET to polyatomic gases, dense gases, and mixtures, and studies global smooth solutions and convergence to equilibrium for symmetric hyperbolic systems with relaxation; the parabolic Navier–Stokes–Fourier theory is the relaxation limit.

**Notable (Jou 2020):** the extended entropy includes explicit flux contributions (ds = T⁻¹du − σ(τ/λT²)q·dq − ...), and the survey explicitly invites social-science application: "the fluxes are nowadays central concepts of our society: fluxes of capital, of matter (fuel, goods), of information... the application of ideas from physics to social sciences is expected to find in extended thermodynamics a possible source of inspiration for the treatment of those fluxes." A direct hook for this program's reception in the thermodynamics community.

**Relevance to program:** Two uses. (1) EIT's phenomenological flexibility is the fallback if the full RET discipline (H2's convex entropy) proves too restrictive for the economic system — one can still build hyperbolic dynamics with a generalized entropy. (2) The RET↔EIT comparison models the kind of "two rationalizations" structure the program finds in value theory (TSSI vs New Interpretation) — methodological sociology of the field. The 2020 comparison paper also catalogs the internal-variables tradition (Maugin) and the GENERIC formalism (Öttinger), further routes to the same end.
