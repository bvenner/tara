# Kliman & McGlone (1999) — A Temporal Single-system Interpretation of Marx's Value Theory

**Citation:** Kliman, A. J., & McGlone, T. (1999). A Temporal Single-system Interpretation of Marx's Value Theory. *Review of Political Economy*, 11(1), 33–59. DOI: 10.1080/095382599107165

**Core claim:** Presents an interpretation of the quantitative dimension of Marx's value theory in which prices and values are determined *interdependently* (single-system) and *within historical time* (temporal). Shows that under this interpretation the standard allegations of internal inconsistency against Marx fail: both aggregate equalities (total price = total value; total profit = total surplus value) can hold simultaneously, the rate of profit can fall, and value is not redundant.

**Key structure:**
- Inputs enter production at time *t* at prices p_t; outputs emerge at *t+1* at prices p_{t+1}. Determination is sequential, not simultaneous: output prices are functions of input prices and current production, not solutions of a static fixed-point system.
- The MELT (monetary expression of labor time), τ_t, converts money sums to labor-time equivalents and is allowed to vary between input and output time.
- Single-system: values and prices are two measures of one system, interdependent — not two separately determined systems (dualism).

**Relevance to program:** Foundational TSSI statement. Supplies the discrete-time difference-equation form of temporal determination that the program recasts as balance laws. The "temporal vs simultaneous" dichotomy it formalizes is the economic pole of the RET analogy.
