# Cattaneo (1948) — Sulla conduzione del calore

**Citation:** Cattaneo, C. (1948). Sulla conduzione del calore. *Atti del Seminario Matematico e Fisico dell'Università di Modena*, 3, 83–101.

**Core content:** The founding move of extended thermodynamics. Fourier's law q = −κ∇T combined with energy balance yields the parabolic heat equation ∂T/∂t = (κ/c_v)ΔT, which propagates disturbances at infinite speed (the "heat paradox"). Cattaneo, reasoning from kinetic arguments, proposed the modified constitutive relation with relaxation:

  τ ∂q/∂t + q = −κ∇T

(with relaxation time τ > 0). Combined with energy balance this yields the **telegraph equation**

  τ ∂²T/∂t² + ∂T/∂t = (κ/c_v)ΔT,

a hyperbolic equation with finite thermal wave speed c = √(κ/(c_v τ)). The parabolic Fourier theory is recovered as the singular limit τ → 0. Experimentally relevant for high-frequency/short-time regimes (second sound in solids and superfluid helium).

**Relevance to program:** The paradigmatic "hyperbolization." The program's Track A applies precisely this move to the capital-flux closure in value-price dynamics: replace the instantaneous response of intersectoral capital flows to profit-rate differentials by a relaxation equation with finite adjustment time. The Cattaneo equation is also the minimal model for the relaxation-limit theorem (H4): τ → 0 collapses the telegraph equation to the heat equation, just as instantaneous adjustment collapses temporal to simultaneous determination.
