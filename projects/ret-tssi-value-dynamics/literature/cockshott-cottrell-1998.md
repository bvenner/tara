# Cockshott & Cottrell (1997/1998) — Does Marx Need to Transform? / Scientific Status of the LTV

**Citations:** Cockshott, W. P., & Cottrell, A. (1998). *Does Marx Need to Transform?* (http://gesd.free.fr/cockcot98.pdf); Cockshott, W. P., & Cottrell, A. (1997). *The Scientific Status of the Labour Theory of Value* (https://users.wfu.edu/cottrell/eea97.pdf); related: Cockshott, Cottrell & Michaelson (1995), "Testing Marx: some new results from UK data," *Capital & Class* 55:103–129.

**Core content:** Empirical program using UK input–output tables: distributions of price/value ratios, profit rates, price/production-price ratios, organic compositions. Findings: simple labor values predict market prices about as well as (or better than) Sraffian prices of production; profit rates correlate *negatively* with organic composition (contrary to the TPP equalization story); the transformation is "incomplete" — both values and production prices act as attractors. Information-theoretic analysis: the Sraffian predictor's extra inputs (capital stocks) buy little information gain; the TSS predictor (requiring input prices) performs worst on parsimony.

**Relevance to program:** (1) Empirical anchor for Track C: input–output tables (now WIOD/OECD ICIO) allow estimation of the model's parameters and the price-value deviation statistics. (2) A substantive puzzle the hyperbolic model can address: partial/incomplete equalization with persistent dispersion is the signature of a *finite-speed* relaxation system under continuous forcing, not of instantaneous equalization — the observed "two attractors" pattern is what one expects mid-relaxation. (3) Their parsimony critique of TSSI is a design constraint: the program's model should add predictive structure (relaxation spectrum), not just extra parameters.
