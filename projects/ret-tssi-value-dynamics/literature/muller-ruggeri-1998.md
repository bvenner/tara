# Müller & Ruggeri (1998) — Rational Extended Thermodynamics (2nd ed.)

**Citation:** Müller, I., & Ruggeri, T. (1998). *Rational Extended Thermodynamics* (2nd ed.), Springer Tracts in Natural Philosophy 37. New York: Springer. DOI: 10.1007/978-1-4612-2210-1

**Core content:** The definitive monograph of RET. Ordinary thermodynamics (Navier–Stokes–Fourier) gives *parabolic* field equations with infinite propagation speeds; extended thermodynamics gives *hyperbolic* systems with finite speeds. Main ingredients:
- Field equations of balance type;
- Constitutive quantities depending on the present local state;
- Entropy as a concave function of the state variables.

These assumptions lead to first-order quasi-linear **symmetric hyperbolic** systems: well-posed Cauchy problems, finite characteristic speeds. Key structural shifts relative to classical irreversible thermodynamics (TIP): entropy depends on non-equilibrium variables (fluxes); entropy flux is a general constitutive quantity (not q/θ); stress and heat-flux equations contain inertial (rate) terms. The state space is *extended*: dissipative fluxes (heat flux, viscous stress) are promoted from constitutive functions to independent fields with their own balance equations (moment hierarchy of the Boltzmann equation in the kinetic case; 13/14-moment and higher closures).

**Relevance to program:** The master reference for the physical pole of the analogy. The program borrows its methodology wholesale: (1) balance laws first; (2) constitutive closure restricted by an entropy principle; (3) fluxes as state variables; (4) convexity ⇒ symmetric hyperbolicity ⇒ well-posedness + finite speed. The book's remark that classical (parabolic) constitutive equations are the zero-relaxation-time approximation of balance laws is the template for H4 (simultaneism as singular limit).
