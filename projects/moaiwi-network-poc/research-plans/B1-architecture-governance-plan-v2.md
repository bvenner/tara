## 1. Scope

What the options memo must answer — **as options with trade-offs, never a recommendation**:

1. A comparative map of candidate architectures for "a corporation that is also a federation of worker cooperatives" (legal form, ownership, control, capital, tax, liability).
2. Governance mechanisms that keep economic democracy structural while admitting investor capital in the adversarial US context — without defaulting to one-member-one-vote.
3. Investor-instrument candidates and the governance consequences of each.
4. Research proposals: the open problems the R&D program should own.

Out of scope: choosing an architecture, drafting legal documents, tax advice. Every legal claim carries a "verify with counsel" flag property.

## 2. Research questions

- **RQ1.** What organizational architectures exist for federations of worker cooperatives (holding cooperative, secondary cooperative, corporation-with-co-op-subsidiaries, multi-stakeholder cooperative, union co-op, contractual network), and what does each imply for capital access, control retention, liability, tax, and formation ergonomics?
- **RQ2.** Which governance mechanisms plausibly hold economic democracy against capital pressure in US legal contexts, and what are their failure modes (capture, co-optation, stagnation, deadlock)?
- **RQ3.** Which investor instruments subordinate capital to mission *as structure rather than promise*, and what does practice show?
- **RQ4.** What does federation-level machinery add over co-op autonomy (Mondragon's inter-cooperation mechanisms; degeneration pressures on federations)?
- *(v2)* **RQ5 (from M3 L455).** How are exit and secession governed — can a thriving unit "go independent," and how are inter-unit transfers (growth funded from profitable parts) kept from breeding resentment?
- *(decided 2026-10-22)* **RQ6.** Should the Corporation concentrate on **software & systems development** or on **direct implementation** (operating, incubating, holding)? Not answerable by argument — it requires **empirical data** from the prototype/fieldwork track before it can be settled; the memo maps what each posture implies for architecture, governance and capital, and leaves the choice open.
