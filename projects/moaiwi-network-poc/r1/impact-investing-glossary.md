# Impact-investing glossary — v1 (working draft for the site)

*A view on an emerging ontology, not a standalone list* (workflow A1). Four columns: **their term** · **definition — quoted or tightly credited to prior art wherever a formal definition exists** · **what using the term commits you to** (the substantive freight the *standard's or movement's usage* drags in beyond the definition) · **our translation** in conventional systems/software-engineering language. Loss markers per the translation-fidelity policy (a)+(b): a lossy rendering carries an explicit `*loses:*` note; plain-English normative phrases stay untranslated. **Register: conventional SE and plain English; internal vocabulary of the research program does not appear here.**

**Sources cited below:** CIDS spec 3.2.1 and introductory guide (Common Approach, `standards-prior-art-memo.md` §2); the Impact Management Norms' five dimensions (Impact Frontiers, via CIDS credits); IRIS+ (via `back-office-needs/landscape-pass1-2026-10-27.md`); USSEN "What We Believe" (ussen.org, CC BY-NC-SA). B2-salvage literature (commensuration, impact-weighted accounts) enters in the commitments column pending direct quotation in v2.

| Their term | Definition (credited) | What using it commits you to | Our translation (SE language) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Impact** | "a change in outcomes for people and the planet" (CIDS intro guide). | That *changes* can be attributed to an organization's work and traced to *outcomes* — i.e., a causal model exists before any number does. Judging the change requires a counterfactual position. | The effect of a system on its environment. **Loses:** the attribution claim — an SE system diagram records coupling, not causation-with-credit. |
| **Outcome** | The condition that changes for people/planet; distinct class in CIDS from outputs/indicators/metrics. | That you can *name* the condition and, in practice, observe it — someone must be able to say what counts. | A defined end-state (or delta) of a system-of-interest, observable in principle. |
| **Indicator** | A preferred or direct measure of an outcome (five-dimensions glossary via CIDS classes). | An observation protocol: data collection, cadence, and someone responsible for the data. | A sensor. Each indicator is a sensor choice with coverage and cost. **Loses:** nothing essential if the honest-conventions caveat travels with it. |
| **Metric (IRIS+ sense)** | A standardized, catalogued indicator — IRIS+ is a taxonomy of standardized metrics aligned "to more than 50 metrics frameworks". **IRIS+ defines what to measure; CIDS defines how to share it** (Common Approach). | Acceptance of a field-standard definition *and its revision process as governing* — except in CIDS's posture, where indicator choice is deliberately **not** standardized. | A protocol/type library for sensors — with a standards body owning the types. |
| **Impact model** (also theory of change, logic model, outcome chain) | "any way that an organization illustrates its impact strategy" (CIDS intro guide); represented as data under CIDS. | That the organization maintains a causal narrative that its measurements are supposed to *instantiate* — and that the narrative is public-enough to be packaged (a capsule). | A system design/requirements document for the change the system is supposed to produce — kept executable (data, not prose). |
| **The five dimensions:** *what, who, how much, contribution, risk* | "(We) understand 'impact' as a change in outcomes for people and the planet. …the five dimensions of impact—what, who, how much, contribution, and risk—plus a sixth dimension of 'how'." (CIDS; norms developed by the Impact Management Project via 2000+ practitioner consultations, housed at **Impact Frontiers**.) | That impact claims are **multidimensional by default** — a single scalar is not the norm's native form; the *norms community itself* treats undimensionalization as a choice needing justification. | A schema: what outcome, for which stakeholders, at what scale, with what attribution, with what failure modes, plus a provenance/quality dimension. **Loses:** practitioner-consultation provenance if stated dryly. |
| **Contribution** (five-dimensions member) | The role the organization played relative to others' effects (field norm). | Fronting the counterfactual: "contribution" concedes others produce similar effects — attribution shrinks to *additionality-plus-comparison*. | Differential or incremental effect vs. baseline/alternatives — i.e., A/B thinking, stated causally. |
| **Impact Measurement and Management (IMM)** | Field term for the practice of measuring *and using* impact data for decisions. | Commitment to **use**, not just report: measurement feeds decisions ( reminds of our diagnostic-not-scoring criterion — measurement must guide improvement). | Observability + control loop: instrumentation with a feed into operating decisions. |
| **Impact data ontology / Common Impact Data Standard (CIDS)** | "A standardized way to represent a social purpose organization's impact model … and the effects of their work on people and the planet"; OWL/JSON-LD; free and public; **does not prescribe indicators** (see A5 memo §2). | Choosing a *shared container* while keeping measurement plural; obliges aligned-tool discipline (export/import via JSON-LD capsules) rather than bespoke ETL per funder. | A data schema + interchange format for the domain — an OpenAPI/JSON-schema-style contract for impact claims. |
| **Impact data capsule** | "your complete impact model … packaged into a single machine-readable file" that "can flow across software systems" (CIDS; aligned-tables pages). | That the SPO owns and packages its own data — the direction of data flow is investee → funder (vs. funder-forms → investee); feasibly, one set of books. A "regulatory-ish" precedent exists: it is required reporting for Canada's Social Finance Fund participants. | A self-contained, portable data export (the "database in a text file") — one authoritative export, many consumers. |
| **Aligned tool / data utility** | Software or spreadsheet-based utilities "embedded" with CIDS structures so practitioners "access the standard without becoming data scientists" (CIDS). | That the *tool vendors* carry the alignment burden, not the SPOs; checking alignment tiers (Basic Tier exists deliberately). | SDK/libraries that implement the format so end-users never see the schema. |
| **Portfolio aggregation / Common Framework** | The Pathfinder pilot created seven portfolio-level frameworks aggregating **1,027 bottom-up metrics** "without prescribing indicators" (Common Approach pilot final report). | That aggregation is *thematic interpretation applied after the fact*, not metric harmonization before it — plural measurement preserved, totals are thematic stories with chosen names (who chose the themes matters; keep the revision question with every aggregate figure). | A dashboarding layer: many heterogeneous sensor streams → named rollups. Every rollup name is a design decision. |
| **Social purpose organization (SPO) / investee** | "an organization with a mission to advance social, cultural, or environmental objectives. These include registered charities, nonprofits, **co-operatives**, hybrid organizations, and private businesses" (Common Approach SFF resources). | Mapping a co-op into the investor's ontological slot: the co-op appears *as an investee* — a role the solidarity-economy vocabulary resists (see below). | The supplier side of the relationship; in our setting, the workplace cooperative. |
| **Solidarity economy** | "a movement and a system built on cooperation, participatory democracy, equity, sustainability, pluralism, community self-determination, and internationalism" (USSEN, credited verbatim). | System replacement, not adjustment: pursuing "a world where we decide… everyone thrives… we cooperate" — commitments about *who decides*, not only about measurement. Using the term aligns you with that movement's aims. | Cooperative economics with explicit governance and equity commitments — a socio-economic design language. **Loses:** its movement character if read as a market segment. |
| **Dual power** | "simultaneously building new [institutions] alongside" existing ones, with the intention that the new replace the old (USSEN). | A theory of change in which the two systems coexist *transitionally*; capital from the old system (impact investors) is being converted toward the new. Explaining it plainly is honest: investors are asked to fund an audience that intends them eventually out of a role, or transformed into it. | Parallel-institution building: run the new system at growing scale inside the old. **Loses:** the conflict framing if softened to "complementary initiatives". |
| **Non-reformist reform** | "a change that actually shifts power… meets immediate needs while building the future at the same time" (USSEN). | Every proposed improvement must be evaluated on two axes: does it *work now* (meets a need) and does it *shift power* (builds the future). Our software offering is pitched against that test, too. | Incremental-but-architectural change: improvements that don't foreclose the restructure. |
| **Power / leverage** (USSEN's own distinction) | "Real power… is the love, dignity, and self-worth we hold together… we organize using our power to achieve leverage" (USSEN). | A deliberate two-vocabulary posture: *power* = collective capacity; *leverage* = positional capability (capital, tools). Investors hold leverage; the circle asks how it compounds the circle's power. | Capability vs. positional advantage (resources, funding, control points). Keep the distinction intact — it is load-bearing for the circle's framing. |
| **Stakeholder** (for contrast) | Common field usage: any party affected by or affecting the venture. | Adopting the field's flattening: affected parties become *roles on a chart*. | Stakeholders — **loses: the standing claim**. The thicker question: which affected public gets to *revise* the measure and when (kept as the standing question on the site, in plain English, by design). |
| **Governance** (for contrast) | Conventional usage: the structures and processes by which an organization is directed. | Neutral adoption hides the authority question. | Governance — **loses: the authority question** — *who* has standing to change the rules is the question the practice keeps open. |

## Loss-marker legend

- `Loses: X` — the nearest conventional-SE rendering is lossy in way X and the loss is marked, not hidden (policy (a)).
- Unmarked plain-English normative phrases ("the affected public", "who chose this, and when does the collectivity get to revise it?") passed through untranslated by policy (b).
