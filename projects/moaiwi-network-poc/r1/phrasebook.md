## 1. From the transcript record (the investors' friends' framing, as reported)

**The shared-back-office idea — their words, not ours:**

-_shader phrasing "having a shared back office, or a shared unified way of getting a software stack back office, is definitely a thing we could do that would save us all a bunch of money" — and the key observation that "they're all spending a bunch of money on this" (MOAIWI-4 :: L57). The scale named at the table: "the ten to twenty investment funds at the table" (L57)._
- **They proposed the research service themselves:** after cross-fund research was raised, "that's immediately what they said… 'oh, this would be a great thing to do, we should probably go ahead and think about allocating budget for a person to work on this'" (MOAIWI-4 :: L31). Reported at L73: "they were immediately, okay, so this is a service we need, how would we allocate money to make sure this person can get paid?" — the willingness-to-pay signal T1 probes.
- **The cross-fund pattern argument, in their language:** one "investment vehicle… this specific group of people, it always goes south" is invisible "inside individual firms who might make two of those investments. But if the entire group has made thirty of those investments, it's very easy to pull that out from the data" (MOAIWI-4 :: L69). Their term for underserved analytics: **"not waste money on a hobby"** (L69) — i.e., funds below scale shouldn't settle for hobby-grade research.
- **The replacement frame for value logic:** a tool "that either replaces existing software purchases or enables impact investors to bring in more money" (MOAIWI-4 :: L27) — the (a)/(b) halves of the meta-plan's value logic stated in their own words at the table.
- **The demo frame:** "a demo of Fortress with the B2B client stuff, and a proposal for the research stack — just imitating… an expandable subset of what already currently exists" and for the longer-term, "we can make vague promises. Making vague promises is fine" (MOAIWI-4 :: L199). *Note.* Under the mutual-education framing we invert the promise style — explicit unknowns replace vague promises (workflow §0 principle 4); the substance of the demo frame is unchanged.
- **Their software-spend shape:** "I would be shocked if those companies are spending $2,500 a year on software" (MOAIWI-4 :: L131) — the skepticism the budget interviews must live with; and the R&D target named: "figuring out what they spend, what they spend it on, and the names of those tools" (L147).
- **Lost opportunity they name for the sector:** the shared back office was raised at the meeting "for around a minute and then dropped it" (MOAIWI-4 :: L57) — a durable opening: the circle's first instinct was cooperation on infrastructure.

## 2. From USSEN's own statement (the circle's host vocabulary)

- **"The system is broken by design. …Small fixes won't solve problems this deep. We need to build something fundamentally new."** (ussen.org "What We Believe" — summary)
- **Foundational values:** "solidarity and cooperation · participatory democracy · sustainability and the rights of Mother Earth · equity in all dimensions · pluralism · community and self-determination · internationalism" (their commitment list).
- **Strategy vocabulary:** **"Build and Fight"** — "we build the new world we want, while we fight to defend our communities and dismantle the old system. Neither one alone is enough." **Dual power** — "we build new economic, political, and social institutions alongside the old ones." **Non-reformist reform** — meets an immediate need "while building the future at the same time."
- **The power/leverage distinction:** "Real power… is the love, dignity, and self-worth we hold together… We organize using our power to achieve leverage." — investors have *leverage*; the network builds *power*. Any pitch that offers "leverage for capital" should be checked against what it does for *power built by those closest to the problems*.
- **Organizational form — the circle itself:** "We organize in circles… Each circle has autonomy to make decisions and move forward, while staying connected to the whole." **Decision rule (consent):** "a proposal is good enough to move forward—as long as no one has a 'serious objection' that would harm the work or our values… if you have a critique or a concern… responsibility lies with those who object" — "If you object, you step up."
- **Whose side:** organizing "led by working-class people, people of color, and those most harmed by the current system," "explicit antiracism and decolonization."

## 3. Consequences for our materials (register discipline, v1)

1. **Lead with their wins:** shared back office, cross-fund research, pattern-finding across "thirty investments" — the research-stack proposal restates *their* framing back to them (workflows C1/T9).
2. **Money language that matches the table:** "replaces existing software purchases" / "allocate budget for a person" — not vendor-speak ("IMM platform," "SaaS enablement"). The gear-system proof for costs stays off the public site until the landscape slice (T1/T2) has interview ground truth.
3. **Consent-decision format for the meeting itself:** the circle's decision rule maps directly onto our D-list format — proposals + questions tolerated + objectors step up; our question list is exactly "showing up to build."
4. **The demo promise discipline:** THEIR instinct here was "vague promises fine" (L199); OUR committed form is explicit unknowns (principle 4). The site carries unknowns in section 5; the talk may soften but must not contradict the site.
5. **Don't adopt the movement vocabulary in the pitch;** respect it as theirs. The glossary keeps the definitions credited; the site's Concepts section speaks plain English (register decision, workflow §0 principle 6 / §6 item 2).
