# Framing memo — moaiwi-network-poc (Phase A)

**Status:** Phase A output → **Gate 1 review item for Nic** · 2026-10-21 · Feeds Phases B–E per the meta-plan.

## 1. Purpose

Fix the pitch's thesis, spine, audience register, and abstraction policy before the research sprints (B) and content assembly (C) harden around them. This memo is the deck's contract: everything later must either conform to it or update it deliberately.

## 2. Pitch thesis

**One sentence:** A corporation that is a federation of worker cooperatives, whose core product is an integrated modeling capability — financial, planetary, and social — that directs capital top-down to compound returns inside the federation while enabling bottom-up formation of cooperatives with genuine, measurable impact stories.

Expanded:

- **Bottom-up pole.** Worker cooperatives form where their members are, with impact stories that are local and concrete: jobs with dignity, ecological repair, community wealth.
- **Top-down pole.** Capital must be directed quickly and at scale, and internal rates of return must grow — otherwise the federation cannot fund formation fast enough to matter.
- **The mediating institution.** The modeling platform translates between the poles. It evaluates and directs capital against all three domains (financial, planetary, social) at multiple scales without collapsing them into one pre-chosen bottom line. Federation-wide, it compounds: every cooperative's data improves allocation for all.
- **The revenue line.** The modeling platform is also a product — served to other cooperative networks, aligned capital, and municipalities — funding the R&D program and reducing dependence on capital returns alone.

## 3. The productive tension (the deck's spine)

Standard models resolve the tension by subordination:

- The conventional holding company gets fast capital and crushes democratic formation.
- The internal-finance cooperative federation (Mondragon's historic path) keeps democratic control and grows capital slowly over decades.

The claim: the tension is not resolved but **made productive** — through the modeling capability and through governance innovation. The pitch presents the tension openly and asks the room to help design the resolution. That request is itself the feedback we came for.

## 4. Why a corporation at all (and why a federation)

- The modeling capability has federation-wide network effects, high fixed costs, and needs sustained R&D; a federation-level entity is its natural carrier.
- Speed: a corporate form can absorb and direct equity capital faster than co-op-by-co-op capital formation.
- Federation: member cooperatives keep their formation energy, their impact stories, and — through governance innovation (B1) — structural influence over the Corporation.
- Legal form is **open** (research output, B1), Colorado-first as working base. "A corporation that is also a federation of worker cooperatives" is the design goal, not a settled statute.

## 5. Audience register (Resist & Build DEN-2026 contacts)

Audience: movement-first, with values-aligned capital in the room (network: USSEN, The Democracy Collaborative, Boston Impact Initiative, Kataly Foundation, People's Network for Land and Liberation, Art.coop, The Guild, Income Movement, Democracy @ Work, Next System Studies; Denver-local: Center for Community Wealth, Cooperation 5280).

Design constraints from the register:

- **Capital is subordinate, visibly.** The instrument and governance designs (B1) exist to make that structural rather than promised. State it early.
- **Name the co-optation risk ourselves.** This network's stated worry is that "building alternatives without roots in our resistance risks leading to more exclusion and co-optation." The plan names the risk and shows where the design answers it: governance innovation (B1), aggregation stance (B2), solidarity-fund mechanics (B3).
- **Language.** Use: economic democracy, solidarity economy, worker self-direction, community wealth, governance innovation, capital that answers to the federation's members, internal rates of return (used honestly, never as the sole good). Avoid: disrupt, exit-as-success, liquidity-event celebration, "human capital", growth-at-all-costs cadence, and any assumed governance template (no a priori one-member-one-vote).
- **IRR is not hidden.** Rapid IRR growth is stated as the mechanism that funds formation at speed — immediately paired with the question this room is best equipped to answer: what constraints must hold so that compounding capital serves economic democracy?

Anticipated objections → prepared posture (responses offered as open, not defensive):

1. *Why investors at all?* Cooperative capital formation is the historical bottleneck. Aligned equity is proposed at speed, with instrument design (B1) as what makes it acceptable. Feedback sought.
2. *Isn't this a holding company with cooperative branding?* The differentiation is the governance innovation program and the open architecture research — offered for co-design, not asserted.
3. *No fixed aggregation scale = unfalsifiable impact?* No: composition rules are explicit per scale, claims are testable per scale, and the demos make composition choices visible and adjustable. Single-score aggregation is the unfalsifiable option.
4. *Who controls the model?* Open. Model and data governance is part of B1; the default direction is that member cooperatives' data stays theirs.
5. *Won't top-down allocation reproduce extractive dynamics?* Allocation and solidarity-fund mechanics are research outputs (B3) informed by network science and cooperative game theory; democratic checks are B1's subject.

## 6. Structural abstraction policy

- **No names.** No corporation name, no cooperative names, no locality beyond "Colorado-first" as jurisdictional working base. Naming is delegated to this audience and future investors as a buy-in mechanism (§7).
- **Role-label glossary** (binding for deck, demos, and memos):

| Label | Means |
|---|---|
| the Corporation | the proposed federation-level entity (legal form open, B1) |
| the Federation | the Corporation plus member cooperatives, considered whole |
| member cooperatives | worker cooperatives participating in the Federation |
| the cohort | the initial set of member cooperatives in the illustrative economics |
| the modeling platform | integrated multi-capital modeling capability (financial + planetary + social) |
| the R&D program | the accounting / governance / business-process research agenda |
| aligned capital | impact investors buying the Corporation's equity instrument (form open, B1) |
| the aggregation stance | the impact-accounting research question (B2) |

- **Options, never decisions:** architecture, governance, instrument, and aggregation formalism appear as researched options with trade-offs (B1/B2), never as commitments.
- **Numbers are illustrative:** every artifact carries the standing disclaimer: *"Illustrative — synthetic federation data. Assumptions documented with the dataset."*

## 7. Naming as engagement

The pitch never proposes a name. The workshop contains a structured naming capture: *"If this existed, what should it be called — and what would the name have to promise?"* Naming input is captured verbatim as buy-in material and brand research for the next phase. The internal working name is not disclosed.
