## N1. The affected public: identification, representation, and voice (methods)
- **Question.** How can the people affected by the federation's decisions — workers, surrounding communities, ecosystems, future generations — be **identified** and **heard in operational ways**, not merely measured? What methods exist and what do they establish: social audit, community benefit agreements, citizen assemblies and juries, participatory action research, stakeholder councils, multi-stakeholder governance forms, Ostrom-style user governance, Indigenous governance traditions?
- **Why it contributes.** The gap between T16's theory and anything buildable. The three-collective map says the affected public's *ekklesia* is missing from Hyphae's stool; T5's metrics are the public's sensory apparatus; the action-circle D3 question is this question asked in public. Without methods, the democracy framing is a slogan — with them, it becomes an engineering requirement. Frame note: this is the **practice stratum** of the Rousseau map (see T13) made deliberate for the terminal object.
- **Informs.** T16, T5, T7 (legal forms for stakeholder voice), Hyphae's product design, the R.1 question list.
- **Priority rationale.** High leverage on the program's coherence; feeds both the Reflect process and product design; much is desk-reachable (methods literature) with fieldwork later.

## N2. Automation, AI, and the cooperative firm
- **Question.** In a worker-owned firm, who decides automation/AI adoption, who captures the gains, and what happens to displaced labor? What do platform-coop/tech-coop histories show? What happens to internal capital accounts, patronage, and hiring when labor can be substituted? At federation scale: does AI concentrate power, or can federations pool it?
- **Why it contributes.** Cooptopia's hypothesis (the ~5% ceiling is a technology/organization limit, not inherent) faces its largest live test — the AI wave may deepen the ceiling before breaking it. The Nic/Brad tension is a special case. Moaiwi/Hyphae will embed AI; governing that embedding is a product requirement.
- **Informs.** T12, Hyphae/Moaiwi product governance, the autonomy program (the constructive-partner question generalized: AI in the regime, not just in our processes).
- **Priority rationale.** Protects the program's future; nearly absent from the current agenda.

## N3. The institutional ecosystem of solidarity-economy support
- **Question.** Who already develops worker co-ops — federations, technical-assistance providers (USFWC, DAWI-type orgs, cooperative development centers, legal clinics like SELC), CDFIs and credit unions, unions, anchor-institution procurement programs (Cleveland/Evergreen model) — and what do they do, charge, need, and fail at? Where are the gaps Hyphae could fill, and who are its natural partners, distributors, and first customers?
- **Why it contributes.** The program knows the literature (T8) and the investor side (T1/T2) but never mapped the living ecosystem the Corporation would join. The replacement process (T11), go-to-market, and interview recruitment all sit inside it. Cheap desk work, immediate practical payoff.
- **Informs.** T8 (contemporary extension), T9, T11, Moaiwi's GTM.
- **Priority rationale.** High practical value, low cost.

## N4. The knowledge commons: IP, licensing, and governance of shared software
- **Question.** How should a federation share one codebase (Fortress → Hyphae) across co-ops while funding development and delivering investor returns? OSS licenses and foundation governance (Apache-style), commons-based peer production, steward ownership of IP, platform-coop precedents, source-available/ethical licenses, PT's role as the developer co-op. **Who owns the means of computation?**
- **Why it contributes.** The locked decisions left "software & systems development vs direct implementation" open pending empirical data — this is that question's home. "Investor–cooperator synergy neither has alone" begs the ownership question. As naming is delegated, IP strategy must be chosen before any business model is promised.
- **Informs.** The long-horizon question, Moaiwi/Hyphae business plans, Cooptopia.
- **Priority rationale.** Decision-forcing before M2/M3 product commitments.

## N5. Cooperative back-office operations (the co-op side of the market)
- **Question.** What do worker-coop administrators and members actually do — payroll, scheduling, bookkeeping, capital accounts, member records, purchasing, patronage distribution? What tools exist, and what breaks at quarter-end?
- **Why it contributes.** T2 studies the investor's back office (Moaiwi's market); Hyphae's actual users are co-ops, unstudied. Plain product-research gap; fieldwork-shaped; can ride the same interviews.
- **Informs.** Hyphae's spec, T6 (observed capital-account mechanics), T11, the Fortress configuration.
- **Priority rationale.** Unblocks Hyphae's requirements; low marginal cost alongside T1/T2 fieldwork.

## N6. Federated data governance: confidentiality, aggregation, explainability
- **Question.** What data may flow between co-ops, to investors, to the public? Commercial confidentiality *inside* a federation; privacy-preserving aggregation; member-level explainability (T14's explainability requirement applied to Hyphae's network model); audit rights and data ownership. Precedents: credit unions, cooperative banking, ESOP reporting, EU/US data law.
- **Why it contributes.** Hyphae's network model requires data co-ops may reasonably refuse to share. Design-load-bearing and currently unowned. Could be folded into T4/T5/T7; proposed as a topic on volume and centrality.
- **Informs.** Hyphae's architecture, T5, T7, the three-collective map.
- **Priority rationale.** Medium; needed before Hyphae's first data model.

## N7. (Optional / lean) The sociology of the impact-investing field
- **Question.** Who are these investors, how do they learn and decide socially, what are the influence networks, and what are the field's own self-critiques (impact-washing debates, the measurement wars)?
- **Why it contributes.** The mutual-education framing makes the audience an object of study. Thin enough to live as questions in T9 (added there 2026-11-01); listed here only in case the empirical ambitions grow.
- **Informs.** T9, T1, the R.1 exchange design.
- **Priority rationale.** Lowest of the seven; questions-embedded by default.
