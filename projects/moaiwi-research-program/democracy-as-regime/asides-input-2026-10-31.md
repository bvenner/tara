## The material (verbatim)

> While meditating last night on "giving and taking of breath", I was struck by the similarities between Brandom's and Castoriadis' frameworks. Brandom focuses on the giving and taking of reasons and Castoriadis on paideia as "a school of governing and being governed" [[castoriadis-1997-summary]]. In the ideal socialist future, these are converging processes, as governance is transformed from the "giving and taking of commands" into the "giving and taking of reasons", which correspond to the distinction between "power over" and "power with".

> Although Castoriadis was a committed athiest, I believe that the framework of "moral universalism" as being developed by Hans Joas (among others) would be more appropriate for describing the "terminal object" than "autonomy". It's a bit of a historical accident that "ekklesia" has acquired a religious aspect under Christianity to mean something like "church", but it seems important to think about the "public" as having a transcendent aspect. For democracy, the "actual" public is extremely important but they should also be considered stewards to a larger "public trust", meaning the entire planet and it's inhabitants. Our Children's Trust attempted to bring the "doctrine" of public trust into a narrow US constitutionalism, with mixed results. MLK used "beloved community" to mean something similar.

> From Gemini: God as terminal object.
> > God is not an entity or creator, but the dynamic unity of all our ideal ends (justice, love, truth) actively working to transform present reality.
