# Reflect process: adopting "democracy as regime" as the terminal object

*Opened 2026-10-25 on Brad's initiative. Trigger: the T3 preliminary findings suggested we may be posing the problem too narrowly — our terminal object ("grow cooperative networks") is **procedural, not substantive**, and by not specifying the substantive goal we may be missing more promising routes to it. Framing source: Castoriadis, "Democracy as Procedure and Democracy as Regime" (1997) — summary in `castoriadis-1997-summary.md`; read as a sketch of **non-anarchist socialism**, against our program's inherited assumptions (solidarity-economy framing; dual-power strategy; the politics/economics division of anarchist socialism / libertarian socialism / social anarchism). Per Brad: this reframing does **not** imply that growing higher-order cooperative networks is not a worthwhile sub-goal, nor that the current program should not be developed — Castoriadis was an early advocate of workplace democracy. This is a Reflect-process document (meta-plan §4.4): its product is a clarified orientation, not a plan.*

## 1. The diagnosis, stated sharply

- **Terminal object (current):** a higher-order network of workplace cooperatives that solves the failures of previous scaling attempts. This names an organizational *form* and a *procedure* of growth. By Castoriadis' criterion it is the economic-democracy analogue of "democracy as procedure": it answers how the economy should be organized, not what collective life is for. The substantive question has been deferred — to the impact vocabulary (environmental/social/financial value), which imports the substance from the impact-investing lexicon rather than from a political conception of the good.
- **Inherited settlement:** solidarity economy + dual power = anarchist-socialist strategy. Its assumptions are ours by default: (i) politics deferred or excluded — build counter-institutions in the economic sphere; the state is the alien power to be defended against; (ii) the politics/economics division taken as given. Castoriadis' refusals: explicit institutions are inescapable ("an absurdity into which both Marx and anarchism lapsed," §I); explicit power is necessary for four reasons no confederation structure dissolves (§I); rights and law are not "formal" screens but substantive terrain (§IV-end).
- **What the T3 taxonomy already showed:** the unaddressed failure modes (FM1 degeneration, FM3 legal-form erosion, FM8 acculturation, FM9 hostile ecosystem) are precisely the political/paideic ones. The evidence was pointing at the reframing before the framing argument arrived.

## 2. Terminal object, restated (draft)

> **The project of an autonomous society in the economic domain and beyond it: explicit and lucid self-institution, as far as possible, of the institutions — economic *and* political — on which collective life depends; individual and collective autonomy; and the common good as the collectivity conceives it in each particular case.** *(Castoriadis §I, §IV, adapted)*

Under this object:

- **Higher-order cooperative networks remain a first sub-goal** — as *paideia* (schools of governing and being governed) and as institutional form, not as the end. The scaling question (T3) becomes: which configurations build autonomy-bearing subjects and institutions, rather than which configurations grow.
- **The substantive question is kept permanently open and permanently asked.** "What is the common good here, now?" is a recurring act of the collectivity concerned — not a metric, not a framework, not a program deliverable. Impact instruments may *serve* this conversation; they cannot *be* it (this reframes T5 and the meta-plan's "measurable impacts").
- **Politics is admitted as program terrain.** Legal form (FM3/FM9), public sphere, and the articulation of oikos / agora / ekklesia are in scope — not "after the economics works." At minimum the program must say what it does about explicit power; at maximum it takes up the institution of society itself. Where on that range we stand is an open question (§4).
- **The politics/economics division is refused as a starting premise.** Economic democracy is a moment of the regime's self-institution — budgets, law, education, and the public sphere included — not a sphere to be built up alongside the polity in dual-power fashion.

## 3. What changes and what does not

| | Current program | Under the reframing |
|---|---|---|
| Terminal object | network growth / solve scaling failures | autonomy — individual + collective; common good conceived by the collectivity |
| Cooperative networks | the end | sub-goal: paideia + form (Castoriadis: early advocate of workplace democracy) |
| Impact measurement (T5) | the substance (value definition) | instrument in service of an open political conversation about the good |
| Politics / law (FM3, FM9) | out of frame; "counsel flags" | in frame; a track to be specified |
| Reflect meta-process | underdeveloped; custodian of "end in mind" | **elevated**: the site where the substantive question is asked each time; paideia becomes program work |
| Moaiwi / Hyphae / Cooptopia | the program | **continue unchanged as work** (explicitly permitted); Hyphae's "three-legged stool" gains a political articulation it currently lacks |
| T3 failure-mode evidence | evidence for the terminal object | evidence about the sub-goal — still valid, differently ranked |

Note the structural echo: the meta-plan's meta-process doctrine (§4) was already more Castoriadian than its terminal object — it names Reflect (the asking of the substantive question) and education-like work as first-class. The reframing makes the two halves consistent.

## 4. Open questions (the work of this Reflect process)

1. **What does "admitting politics" concretely mean** for a two-person cooperative + LLMs? Options range from (a) consciousness/education work (paideia inside the products and the cooperatives), through (b) institutional design work (legal forms, the federalism-vs-confederalism question now flagged in the topics doc), to (c) explicit engagement with public power (policy, the state, municipal/regional politics). The dual-power inheritance made (c) taboo; the reframing reopens it.
2. **Who is "the collectivity concerned"?** Castoriadis' common good is conceived by a concrete collectivity. Ours is currently diffuse (users, investors, cooperators, the action circle, USSEN). The reframing forces a decision about *which* collectivity does the conceiving — and gives the answer to "who names the projects" (naming policy, meta-plan §2) a deeper meaning.
3. **Does the 5% ceiling question (Cooptopia/T12) change rank?** Under the regime framing, the ceiling may be a symptom of the political settlement rather than a technological limit (T3 finding FM9: jointly institutional and technological) — which would make Cooptopia partially a political project or dissolve it into one.
4. **How to keep the program honest between the two failures:** proceduralization (impact metrics standing in for the good — the Castoriadis condemnation) on one side, and philosopher-king behavior (we define the good for the collectivity — the Platonic error §IV names) on the other. Candidate discipline: every substantive yardstick the program adopts carries a standing question — *who chose this, and when does the collectivity get to revise it?*
5. **Relationship to the meta-plan.** Does meta-plan v3 §1's "end in mind" get restated in v3 (revision), or does this live as a parallel Reflect document that v3 references? Brad's call; the safe default is a referenced note until the Reflect process produces a settled formulation.
