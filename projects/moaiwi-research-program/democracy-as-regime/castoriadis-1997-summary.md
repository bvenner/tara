# Castoriadis 1997 — summary and reading against our program

*Source: Cornelius Castoriadis, "Democracy as Procedure and Democracy as Regime" (lecture Rome 1994 / Columbia 1995; written version in* La Montée de l'insignifiance *(Seuil 1996); trans. David Ames Curtis). Full text: `corpus/fulltext/castoriadis_1997_democracy_procedure_regime.md`, PDF at <https://mirror.explodie.org/castoriadis1997.pdf>. Retrieved and converted 2026-10-25 at Brad's request, as the sketch of an approach to **non-anarchist socialism**. Section pointers below (§I–V) are the essay's own.*

## 1. The argument in one page

**The target.** A conception of democracy, "breaking with all previous political thought," that makes it "a mere set of 'procedures'" (opening). Political thought always saw democracy as a **regime** — indissociable from a substantive conception of the ends of the political institution and of the type of human being that corresponds to it. Proceduralism is not a neutral clarification: it "originates in the crisis of the imaginary significations that concern the ultimate goals of collective life and aims at covering over this crisis" by dissociating all discussion of goals from the form of the regime — "ultimately, even by eliminating the very idea of such goals."

**The anthropology behind it.** There is no presocial individual; the "individual-substance" fiction is the shared foundation of procedural democracy and of *homo oeconomicus* (§I; §III on *homo juridicus* as equally fictitious). Society is institutions + social imaginary significations; power is ineliminable — an implicit "infrapower" always, and explicit sanction-bearing power necessarily (four reasons: the presocial world threatens meaning; other societies exist; the psyche is never fully socialized; the future cannot be exhaustively codified) (§I).

**The line that matters most for us.** "There can be, there has been, and we hope there again will be societies without a State… A society without such a State is possible, conceivable, and desirable. **But a society without explicit institutions is an absurdity into which both Marx and anarchism lapsed**" (§I).

**The constructive definitions.** Politics = "explicit and lucid activity that concerns the instauration of desirable institutions"; **democracy = "the regime of explicit and lucid self-institution, as far as is possible, of the social institutions that depend on explicit collective activity"** (§I) — a movement, not a perfect society: the project of an **autonomous society**. Its objective is not happiness but **freedom = autonomy**, and individual and collective autonomy are mutually inconceivable: I am free under the law only if I had effective part in positing it, so "the autonomy of all is and has to be a fundamental concern of each" (§II). Liberty and equality therefore imply each other; the supposed opposition is a fabrication (§II n.6).

**Why procedure alone cannot do it.** (a) Formal/procedural guarantees (rights, due process, separation of powers) are "strictly defensive" — they presuppose an alien power facing society, the tacit philosophy of the Commons *versus* the King (§II). (b) "It is therefore not possible to achieve even a 'procedural democracy' that is not a fraud unless one intervenes deeply in the substantive organization of social life" (§II). (c) Every decision is substantive in fact: no law avoids substance (even the murder prohibition needs qualifications), the judge cannot be a *Paragraphenautomat* (equity — Plato/Aristotle's inadequacy of the universal form of law to the singular case), government is necessarily "arbitrary" (the law never says what belongs in a budget) (§III). (d) Proceduralism therefore smuggles in two substantive judgments: that existing institutions are compatible with democratic procedure, and that individuals as society fabricates them can run it (§III). (e) Every procedure is applied by humans who must apply it "in its spirit" — hence **paideia** is ineliminable: "There can be no democratic society without democratic paideia" (§III). And the education dilemma: if education is dogmatic, democracy is "the political equivalent of a religious ritual"; if it is critical, "the Pandora's box of putting existing institutions into question is opened up and democracy again becomes society's movement of self-institution — that is to say, a new type of regime" (§III).

**The three spheres.** *Oikos* (private) / *agora* (private-public) / *ekklesia* (public-public = explicit power) — "clearly distinguished and properly articulated only under a democratic regime" (§III). Today's pseudodemocracies have "rendered the public sphere in large part private: the decisions that really count are those made in secret or behind the scenes." A definition of democracy as good as any: "the regime in which the public sphere becomes truly and effectively public." Athenian procedures (rotation, sortition, deliberation, popular courts) were not means but **moments of an active paideia** (§III).

**Common good, happiness, living well.** Against Berlin: happiness is private (*eudaimonia*, "not eph' hēmin"); the common good / living well (*eu zēin*) is public and "in great part depends on us"; **"the realization of the common good is the condition for living well"** (§IV). No philosophy can define happiness or the substantive common good for everyone; no politics can wait for one to do so. The common good is created each time **social-historically by the collectivity** (not theorized), and its nonrelative component is: "The city must do everything possible to aid citizens in becoming effectively autonomous" (paideia). Hence the closing definition: **democracy as regime "tries to achieve, as far as it possibly can, both individual and collective autonomy and the common good such as it is conceived by the collectivity concerned in each particular case"** (§IV).

**Against the Marxist "merely formal" dismissal.** Bourgeois rights/liberties did not arise with capitalism, are not its spirit ("the latter demands… the Taylorist 'one best way' or the 'iron cage'"), are **not formal but "vitally necessary traits of every democratic regime"** — partial and essentially defensive — and their denunciation "served as a springboard for the instauration of Leninist totalitarianism" (§V area / §IV-end). The essay closes on democratic politics as lucid action that reduces the contingency of our social-historical existence (§V).

## 2. The reading Brad proposed: substitute "economic democracy" for "democracy"

Do this and the essay becomes an implicit critique of our program as presently stated. The correspondences:

1. **Our terminal object is procedural.** "Grow higher-order cooperative networks" states a *form* of economic organization — exactly the structure of "democracy as procedure." The essay's opening charge applies verbatim: we have dissociated the discussion of ultimate goals from the form of the organization and may be "eliminating the very idea of such goals." The meta-plan's "end in mind" (a network that solves scaling failures) answers *how the economy should be organized*, not *what collective life is for*.
2. **We inherited the anarchist-socialist settlement.** The solidarity-economy framing and the dual-power strategy carry its assumptions: politics is either excluded or deferred (build counter-institutions in the economic sphere; the state is the alien power to be defended against — §II's exact description of the tacit procedural philosophy), and the politics/economics division is taken as given. Castoriadis' hit is direct: a society without explicit institutions "is an absurdity into which both Marx and anarchism lapsed" (§I), and explicit power is *necessary* for four reasons that no confederation structure dissolves (§I). A program that builds in the *agora* while leaving the *ekklesia* unaddressed has accepted the very division the regime concept refuses.
3. **The impact agenda is a substantive-claims problem.** "Environmental, social and financial value" is our yardstick of the substantive good — imported from the impact-investing lexicon, i.e. externally measured. Castoriadis: substantive goods are created each time by the collectivity concerned; measurement cannot substitute for the political question (this sharpens T5: metrics can *serve* a collectivity's conception of living well but cannot *be* it).
4. **FM8 in the T3 findings is this essay's thesis in sociological dress.** The critical-theory paper's acculturation finding (dominant modes of thought/desire defeat degeneration's opponents) converges with paideia: structure does not produce democratic subjects; institutions of education do. Our only named countermeasure to FM8 was "Reflect" — under Castoriadis, paideia is not a meta-process garnish but the Archimedean point of the whole project (§II: "the Archimedean point here is obviously *paideia*").
5. **What the essay does *not* say.** It does not devalue workplace democracy — Castoriadis was among its early advocates. The cooperative network remains valuable *as paideia*: a school of governing and being governed (Aristotle's citizen), and a school of autonomy. What changes is its rank: from terminal object to one moment of a larger project of autonomy that explicitly includes politics.

## 3. What an alternative program would have to do differently

Sketch territory for the Reflect process (opened in `reflect-terminal-object-sketch-2026-10-25.md`):

- **Terminal object restated:** the project of an autonomous society — explicit and lucid self-institution of economic *and political* institutions, individual and collective autonomy, the common good as the collectivity conceives it each time. Cooperative networks become a sub-goal (paideia + substantive institutional form), not the end.
- **Politics admitted:** an explicit track on power, law, and public sphere — the things dual-power strategy leaves to "later." At minimum: what legal/institutional forms (FM3/FM9 territory) and what public-sphere work the program undertakes.
- **The division refused:** economic democracy treated as part of the regime's self-institution (budgets, law, paideia, oikos/agora/ekklesia articulation), not a separate sphere to be built up alongside.
- **Substantive questions kept permanently open:** the good is conceived by the collectivity each time — so the program's substantive yardsticks (impacts, value) are instruments in service of an ongoing political conversation, never its substitute. This is where "Democracy as procedure" would condemn us: for proceduralizing the *substance* (impact metrics) while romanticizing the *form* (network growth).
- **What survives untouched:** Moaiwi/Hyphae/Cooptopia development continues (explicitly permitted by the reframing); T3's failure-mode evidence stands as evidence about the sub-goal; the research machinery (TARA, corpus, meta-processes) is reusable — indeed the meta-process doctrine is *closer* to Castoriadis than the terminal object was, since it names reflection and education as first-class work.
