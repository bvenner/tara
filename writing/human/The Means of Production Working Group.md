# The Means of Production Working Group Proposal

**Status:** draft proposal for a Denver DSA working group.
**Relationships:** this project is focused on economics with a Marxist flavor but is independent of the `ret-tssi-value-dynamics` and `island-digital-twin` research threads.

---

## Big Problem

After talking to members of the Denver cooperative community, the main reason companies do not incorporate as worker cooperatives is a **dearth of politically conscious individuals** who have an idea for a company, collaborators to start it with, and the support from a community to get it off the ground.

**Research support (why this diagnosis is right):** the empirical literature agrees that worker cooperatives are *not* rare because they fail — research over the last decade finds worker-managed firms survive at least as long as, and often longer than, conventional firms (lower hazard of dissolution; Burdín 2014 found a ~29% lower hazard for worker-managed firms in Uruguay; Olsen 2013 surveys US/UK/Israeli cohorts with the same conclusion; the 2019 US worker-cooperative census finds mature worker co-ops out-survive comparable small businesses). The 2021 census found the sector grew ~30% in two years to 612 businesses and that co-ops weathered COVID better than conventional firms. The scarcity of worker cooperatives therefore traces to **low formation rates**, not to competitive viability. The formation obstacles the literature names — wealth and credit constraints, collective-action problems, and the difficulty of finding co-founders — are exactly the diagnosis above: few politicized people with an idea, collaborators, and community support.

A crucial detail: **most US worker co-ops (~69%) are start-ups, not conversions** (Denmark/US census; 2019). That means the "ideation + founding team + community" problem this group attacks is the *dominant* entry mode, not a side path.

## Proposed solution

Start a "Means of Production Working Group." It will likely start as a series of workshops and eventually evolve into a full working group.

**Hosting:** hosted by the Denver chapter of the Democratic Socialists of America (https://www.denverdsa.org/). A local copy of the chapter's new-member handbook (which defines working groups, and includes a "Means of Production" glossary entry used for member education) is at `papers/incoming/DDSA_Member_Handbook.pdf`.

## Format

Most lectures will be presented by a combination of (1) a researcher and (2) someone who has a job in a productive sector of the economy. The practitioner's lecture will be a description of what their job is, what they actually produce for customers, what they charge their customers routinely, what individual workers are paid, and other statistics such as the ratio of worker earnings to company revenue — i.e. real production data, not abstract theory.

## Goal

The workshop is meant to eventually serve as an **ideation hotspot and networking opportunity**, which will encourage the formation of companies and workplace cooperatives. The community that forms can become a place where individuals can get initial customers and other small bits of help.

## Big Problem #2: how do you make this an engaging thing that will actually make people want to show up?

- Provide food.
- Focus on sectors that are interesting to DSA: **housing, healthcare, transportation, food**. (These map to the chapter's own stated commitments — healthcare, housing, a living wage — in the member handbook.)

## Why Denver, why now (evidence annex for the proposal)

- **Denver has a real, small worker-cooperative ecosystem to connect into** (not to duplicate): the Rocky Mountain Employee Ownership Center (since 2012; incubation, education, conversions, co-founded Drivers Coop Colorado, now a ~1,000-member worker-owned rideshare), the Center for Community Wealth (cooperative academies, a state-wide worker-coop peer network, + the City of Denver Business Cooperative Development Program), and a Colorado employee-ownership office at OEDIT. The proposed group complements rather than competes: the ecosystem lacks an *ideation and founding-team pipeline* — exactly the empty slot this working group fills.
- **Denver is a national leader in employee ownership policy** yet worker co-ops number only ~40 statewide (2025–26 CCW report); the bottleneck is formation (above), which is the group's purpose.
- **Sector precedents already exist locally** (transportation: Drivers Coop; food; home care; ecological landscaping; childcare), so sector-focused lectures can be concrete and grounded.
- **DSA-specific fit:** worker-cooperative formation is an established, argued priority in DSA (e.g. DSA national blog, "Build Socialist Enterprises" 2018; Democratic Socialist of America focus on democratizing the workplace; other chapters run worker-coop workshops). The name "Means of Production" already appears in the DDSA member handbook glossary, so the framing is native to the organizing vocabulary.

## Research support — sources

- Erik Olsen, "The Relative Survival of Worker Cooperatives and Barriers to Their Creation" (2013), *Advances in the Economic Analysis of Participatory & Labor-Managed Firms* — co-ops survive at/above conventional firms; rarity = low formation (credit constraints, collective action, co-founders).
- Gabriel Burdín, "Are Worker-Managed Firms More Likely to Fail Than Conventional Enterprises?" (2014, Uruguay panel) — ~29% lower dissolution hazard.
- U.S. Federation of Worker Cooperatives, *Worker Cooperative Economic Census* (2019 update) — survival advantage, pay-ratio findings, ~69% startup / ~24% conversion origin.
- Democracy at Work Institute & USFWC, *2021 Worker Cooperative State of the Sector* — 612 businesses, ~30% growth, COVID resilience.
- Center for Community Wealth (Denver), *State of the Denver Worker Cooperative Sector* (2025–26) — ~40 worker co-ops in Colorado, Front Range concentration.
- Rocky Mountain Employee Ownership Center: rmeoc.org (incubation, education, legacy/conversion work, Drivers Coop).
- City of Denver Economic Development & Opportunity, Business Cooperative Development Program (cooperative education + incubation, with Center for Community Wealth).
- Colorado OEDIT Office of Employee Ownership (state-level technical support and tax incentives).
- DSA national: "Build Socialist Enterprises" (dsausa.org, 2018); DDSA member handbook (local copy at `papers/incoming/DDSA_Member_Handbook.pdf`).
