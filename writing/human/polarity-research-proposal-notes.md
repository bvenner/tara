# String diagrams as syntax

We have recently been working on two research topics in the moaiwi-resesearch-program project: developing an "autonomy" program to guide the long-term evolution of the "solidarity economy" at [democracy-as-regime](./projects/moaiwi-research-program/democracy-as-regime/) and a second research topic that is still being developed in this reflect process: developing an explicit knowledge base (however named) as a formal output of a research process that could be used in a develop process as sketched in the research topic [research-topics-v3.md#T18](./projects/moaiwi-research-program/research-topics-v3.md#T18), titled "neurosymbolic knowledge engineering". Currently, TARA is using an off-the-shelf hypergraph library written in Python as it's explicit knowledge base.  which would hopefully help understand and develop a useful approach to explicit knowledge that would complement LLM's and address the needs of a develop process grounded in a vericoding approach (as realized in KVerus, for example). At the core of this proposed topic is Bradley Allen's Elenchus program, which attempts to base a knowledge engineering process on Robert Brandom's book "Reasons for logic, logic for reasons". Elenchus uses non-monotonic multiple-sequent logic to develop a "material base."

Although there are some similarities between hypergraphs and a material base, there are important differences. Hypergraphs have boxes that have multiple inputs and multiple outputs and a naive reading could simply identify a hypergraph node with a "material implication.". However, the two logics have important differences and do not obviously have the same semantics. So one question is whether there is a "string diagram" doctrine that corresponds to NMMS? The series of papers "String Diagram Rewrite Theory I, II, III" could serve as a basis for such a search. 

Another potential larger space to look for string diagram semantics would be Myer's double operadic systems theory, which develops a theory of "flavored Petri nets" that could have a useful diagrammatic syntax, even if not an SMC exactly since this depends upon double categories. Since an SMC is a special type of double category, it is not necessarily exclusive, though. A double operadic systems theory is a "symmetric monoidal loose right module" over a "symmetric monoidal double category", so this theory appears to be more complex than the string diagram rewrite theory. Since string diagrammatic rewriting can be thought of as cospans of symmetric monoidal categories, there is a parallel here (symmetric monoidal loose right module -> cospan, symmetric monoidal double category -> SMC). One could argue that since a symmetric monoidal double category is a special type of a triple category in the same way that a symmetric monoidal category is a special type of double category, surface diagrams would be a more appropriate category of syntax than string diagrams. Since string diagram rewriting theory is almost certainly more close to implementation than a surface diagram rewriting theory, it makes sense to work with string diagrams until their limits are reached, while keeping in mind their potential limitations as a general systems theory.

Kris Brown developed a categorical semantics for NMMS that does not obviously correspond to an SMC (although this is itself a research question), so has this question already been answered in the negative. Brown looks towards linear logic as inspiration to the multiple sequents on the left and the right in a material base and the fact that in classical logic the "tensor product" is different in either side of a sequent (multiplication on the left and addition on the right). Classical logic closely identifies these through negation but linear logic relaxes this negation.   

Would it be better to simply adopt the "knowledge engineering/material base" framing and use tools such as pyNMMS without a categorical semantics? 

# TARA as a records management system

I'd like to develop a research topic that is focused on improving the current realization of the research process in TARA. As such, this topic may belong in another project (for example knowledge-repository-poc). TARA itself should not be subject to a complete rewrite until the Moaiwi develop and deploy processes are more explicitly realized, as a research assistant is a software product and thus subject to a develop and deploy process. However, as sketched below, I believe that there are changes that could be made that would retain much of the current architecture but would make the products of the research process more "future proof".

One obvious target to make TARA outputs more future proof is to select a durable, widely-recognized format to preserve TARA outputs. This could make the research process less dependent on actually developing TARA and would allow focusing developer resources on more important tasks (i.e. tasks that are unlikely to be implemented by other groups and are high-leverage towards realizing the "autonomy terminal object"). As discussed in ./archive/projects/anytype-based-tara, I previously thought about using AnyType for this durable format but abandoned this approach. Nic had mentioned using Obsidian rather than AnyType, and this was one factor in my decision to abandon AnyType development.

Andrej Karpathy has developed a high-level overview on using LLMs to assisted in developing a wiki that leverages Obsidian and can be used as a personal knowledge management system at https://gist.github.com/karpathy/442a6bf555914893e9891c11519de94f. This has a number of parallels with TARA as currently implemented, but implements an Obsidian-driven workflow that resembles my proposed AnyType-based approach. However, Karpathy's proposal is more well developed than my AnyType system proposals and outlines not just a structure but a workflow. The instructions section above shows that it could be considerably easier for a human to write comments on the repository if the repository was structured more like a wiki.

On the other hand, I believe the use of hypergraphs in TARA is a potential strength and has the potential to be even more powerful if the string diagram research project outlined above bears fruit. One way that the hypergraph system could be used would be to target TARA as an ISO 15489 records management system that uses hypergraphs to keep track of record metadata. This would leverage existing work on using Obsidian in records management. One step in this process requires generating records metadata when a file is first created. However, ISO 15489 also requires tracing records over an entire records lifecycle, and it may be more appropriate to track this lifecycle metadata outside of the file contents, so that the record can be treated as an immutable objects and could be managed as such. Since relational databases can be considered as a hypergraph, this approach is not conceptually very different from existing records management systems that uses a relational database to manage record metadata.

I see some additional advantages to a hypergraph-based system to complement a wiki. First, the hypergraph can be maintained as a text file that is managed in the same git respository as the documents, as currently implemented in TARA. Second, the hypergraph can be represented as a string diagram as discussed in the string diagram section, which allows for visualizing the hypergraph structure that uses nesting to avoid the extremely complex directed graph "spaghetti" that can be produced by Obsidian. Such visualizations could leverage hypergraph network software and other network science tools that are already on the Moaiwi roadmap. Third, we have already seen the potential benefits of the hypergraph-based approach in developing concept maps and grounded research summaries in projects such as ./project/agent-harness-poc and I believe that this work shows some potential promise if TARA uses the hypergraph to manage it's own records, not only academic documents produced outside the system.

# Asides

While meditating last night on "giving and taking of breath", I was struck by the similarities between Brandom's and Castoriadis' frameworks. Brandom focuses on the giving and taking of reasons and Castoriadis on paideia as "a school of governing and being governed" [castoriadis-1997-summary](./projects/moaiwi-research-program/democracy-as-regime/castoriadis-1997-summary.md). In the ideal socialist future, these are converging processes, as governance is transformed from the "giving and taking of commands" into the "giving and taking of reasons", which correspond to the distinction between "power over" and "power with".  

Although Castoriadis was a committed athiest, I believe that the framework of "moral universalism" as being developed by Hans Joas (among others) would be more appropriate for describing the "terminal object" than "autonomy". It's a bit of a historical accident that "ekklesia" has acquired a religious aspect under Christianity to mean something like "church", but it seems important to think about the "public" as having a transcendent aspect. For democracy, the "actual" public is extremely important but they should also be considered stewards to a larger "public trust", meaning the entire planet and it's inhabitants. Our Children's Trust attempted to bring the "doctrine" of public trust into a narrow US constitutionalism, with mixed results. MLK used "beloved community" to mean something similar.
