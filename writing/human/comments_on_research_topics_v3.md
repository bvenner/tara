## Would a reflect process applied to the meta-processes themselves be appropriate?

There are several research topics that relate to the larger goals of developing the "meta-processes infrastructure". Because of the inter-dependence of the meta-processes, it can be difficult to develop an appropriate sequence of topics. I would classify the topics RT.(T15, T13, T17, T14, T18) as primarily meta-process focused. I believe it might be best to develop a "meta-process-research-plan" that would frame the problem at a high level and for the plan to acknowledge the mutually recursive nature of the ideas in these topics. Since the current convention for a reflect process is to generate a meta-plan output, I suggest a reflect process on the meta-processes themselves prior to more formal specification of specific research plans under these topics. A key question is how can generative AI be included as a constructive partner to these processes. There is a coalgebra-like circularity here, with X = reflect process, F(X) is a specification of the four meta-processes (including the reflect process), and X -> F(X) the coalgebra-like circularity.

Some particular questions.
1. How does the research meta-process relate to conventional systems and software engineering? What are the inputs to a research process (in addition to outputs from a reflect process) and what are the outputs (what does a develop process need from a research process). 

2. What are the differences, if any, between "knowledge engineering", "research meta-process" and "requirements engineering".

3. If a reflect process surfaces tacit knowledge into natural language, should the research process refine these natural language requirements into a "material base (Elenchus)", and would this make "knowledge engineering" a combination of the two processes?

4. Would a structured dialogue process, argumentation framework or multi-agent debate mediated by an LLM as described in the Elenchus program be an appropriate framing for a reflect process, or would this be too rigid and formal? How could conflict between multiple users or experts be resolved? 

5. How does the non-monotonic multi-succedent logic used in Elenchus relate to other aspects of the program, such as categorical logic, categorical systems theory or higher-order networks? Informal descriptions of logical expressivism bring to mind pre- and post-conditions but the semantics presented by Brown seem far from this. A closer link between these two approaches would be very valuable as a way to systematically relate vericoding with requirements engineering.

6. What existing tools implement NMMS? Brown has developed ROLE.jl and Allen has developed pyNMMS. Are there other implementations? Brown has not worked on ROLE.jl in over a year.
