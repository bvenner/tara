# Building a community knowledge system using Atproto

The AT Protocol is the protocol underlying the Bluesky social network. There is a community of developers that are building an "ecosystem" of applications that run on this protocol. Many of these applications have potential for use in a social knowledge tool and there are hints that these tools could work together to enable the sort of AI-assisted wiki workflow advocated by (Karpathy)[[https://gist.github.com/karpathy/442a6bf555914893e9891c11519de94f]. In fact, Erlend Sogge Heggen at blog.muni.town advocated the idea of a (chatty community digital garden)[https://blog.muni.town/chatty-community-gardens/] as an emergent design paradigm where community digital gardens emerged from a community forum and suggested that a more formal wiki structure could serve as an intermediate state between "personal chaos" and a "structured article". Heggen also seemed to suggest that a structured process could be used to bridge the gap between a chat, a wiki, and an article.

Heggen links to an article by Maggie Appleton tracing the idea of a (digital garden)[https://maggieappleton.com/garden-history]. Although this article is focused on "personal knowledge", it uses an organic metaphor for what Habermas might call a "lifeworld of knowledge" that is different from the more inorganic concepts of "system" or "management". This metaphor echoes Peirce's idea that "concepts grow" and his description of a continous system of transformations on concepts, spreading out and connecting to other concepts is a network that is more neural than ordered. Ideally, the term "knowledge governance" should be neutral between organic and inorganic approaches, much as the intended use of "cybernetics", but there seems to be an irresistible pull towards inorganic concepts unless deliberately introduced. 

The ongoing tension between "organization" and "governance" is apparent here, as well as the internal tension introduced by the "self" modifier. Both terms tend towards dualism - an organizer and what is organized, a governor and what is governed. I've followed the general trend in the physics literature to refer to "self-organized" systems as lacking an internal governance system as well as Jenann Ismael's excellent essay on this topic.
