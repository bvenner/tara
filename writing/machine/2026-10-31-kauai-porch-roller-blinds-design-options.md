# Heavy-Duty Outdoor Roller Blinds — Kauai Screened Porch

Design options for retractable weather blinds on a second-story screened porch,
Kauai. All dimensions are approximate and must be field-verified before ordering.

## 1. Site and constraints

| Parameter | Value (approx.) | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Knee wall height | 1.0 m | approx. |
| Ceiling height | 3.0 m | approx. |
| Blind drop (ceiling to knee-wall cap) | 2.0 m | derived |
| Side openings (x2) | 3.0 m W x 2.0 m H = 6.0 m² | approx. |
| Center opening (x1) | 6.0 m W x 2.0 m H = 12.0 m² | approx. |
| Total fabric area | 24.0 m² (258 sf) | derived |
| Location | Second story, fully exposed, over living space | — |
| Environment | Salt air, high UV, high humidity, trade winds, vog, hurricane zone | — |
| Prior event | ~100 mm (4 in) standing water in porch during storm | — |

Priority order stated by owner: **view > rain exclusion > hurricane
protection > shade.**

## 2. Diagnosis

1. The fixed insect screens pass essentially all wind-driven rain. They are an
   insect filter, not a rain screen.
2. The ~100 mm pool was a **drainage failure**, not a coverage failure. The
   knee wall plus screen sill forms a basin whose only outlet is the door.
   At 100 mm over a ~12 x 20 ft porch that is ~600 gal / ~5,000 lb on a
   second-story floor.
3. **A blind mounted inboard of the screens will not exclude driving rain.**
   It intercepts what the screen passes and delivers it, concentrated, to the
   inside base of the knee wall.
4. No fabric system is a hurricane barrier. Zip-track shades top out around
   80–100 mph and are specified to be retracted in storms.

## 3. Decisive geometry: the fabric plane

Rule: **the bottom bar must land outboard of the knee wall's outer face**, on a
cap sloped ~1/8 in/ft outward with a drip edge and weeps.

This implies mounting the cassette on the **outer face of the frame**, fabric
falling in the outermost plane, with the existing fixed screens retained
behind it. The screens do not need to be converted to removable.

Trade-offs of the outboard mount:

- Cassette and fabric take full salt and UV — must be marine-grade.
- Cassette is visible from outside.
- A 10%-openness mesh in front of 18-mesh screen can produce **moiré** in
  bright light. Test with samples before ordering (see §5).

Inboard mounting is acceptable only as a shade/privacy device, not as rain
control.

## 4. Layering strategy

| Layer | Device | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Outer | Removable rigid storm panels, or roll-down aluminum shutter | Hurricane + storm rain |
| Shade plane | Zip-track mesh shade, outboard of screen | Daily rain attenuation, glare, privacy, wind stability |
| Inner | Existing fixed screens (retained) | Insects |
| Envelope | Sloped deck, scuppers, membrane, extended overhang | The standing-water event |

## 5. Fabric selection

| Candidate | Openness | Rain | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Serge Ferrari Soltis 92 | ~10% | partial (~60–75% attenuation, est.) | PVC-coated, antifungal, 10-yr; default |
| Serge Ferrari Soltis 86 | ~14% | partial, less | More view, less glare control |
| Serge Ferrari Soltis Proof 502 | 0% | full | Waterproof + antifungal, but **opaque** — defeats the view priority |
| Textilene 90 | ~10% | partial | Cheaper alternative |
| Sunbrella / solution-dyed acrylic | varies | poor | Porous; permanent mildew staining. Avoid |

Requirements: heat-welded edges, heat-welded zip, no open hem pocket, welded
seams (no needle holes below the water line). 1800 mm roll goods imply one
vertical seam per 3 m module.

**Mandatory pre-purchase test:** obtain 300 mm samples of Soltis 92 and Soltis
86; tape over one opening; inspect at midday for moiré and view quality. This is
the highest-value step in the project.

## 6. Bay-by-bay specification

| | Qty | Size | Area | Cassette | Drive |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Side openings | 2 | 3.0 x 2.0 m | 6.0 m² | 100 mm powder-coated Al | Crank |
| Center opening | 1 | 6.0 x 2.0 m, as 2 x 3.0 m modules | 12.0 m² | 100 mm x 2 | Crank (motor optional) |

**Split the 6 m opening into two 3 m modules.** Reasons: 1.8 m fabric widths
require seams anyway; a 6 m span needs a reinforced tube and dual synchronized
motors to avoid center sag; two 3 m cassettes ship in 10-ft crates instead of
oversize freight to Kauai; halves the weight to hang; allows single-unit
replacement. Cost: one slim vertical break at the 3 m line.

**Crank, not motor, for the side bays.** Eliminates the electrician, the GFCI at
the ceiling, salt-air motor failure, and the controller-logic problem in §8.

### Wind-load sanity check (for the contractor)

- q = 0.00256 x V²; 100 mph → 25.6 psf; x1.1 component factor ≈ 28 psf.
- Side bay (64.6 sf): ~1,810 lb total; ~900 lb transferred to cassette over
  9.84 ft ≈ **90 lb/ft**.
- Center module: same per module.
- At Kauai ultimate (130 mph, q ≈ 43 psf, x1.1): side bay ~3,080 lb.
- Attachment: continuous blocking or steel angle; 8–10 fasteners per cassette;
  316 stainless only.

## 7. Hurricane layer — risk decision, not a code requirement

Kauai effective ultimate wind speed is 130+ mph and the county treats it as a
windborne-debris region, but opening protection is required for *enclosed
habitable space*. An open screened porch is not that.

Exposure: the porch floor is over living space. A wet floor without a membrane
becomes a ceiling repair.

| Option | Cost (24 m²) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Roll-down aluminum shutter (crank) | $10,000–15,500 | Near-watertight, operated from inside, no ladder. **Recommended** given second-story access |
| Removable rigid panels (polycarbonate/ACM, SS studs) | ~$3,000–5,000 DIY | Requires leaning out a second-story opening to deploy — awkward and unsafe |
| Accept the risk | $0 | Defensible **only** with a properly waterproofed and drained floor |
| "Hurricane screen" fabric products | varies | Wind/debris rated, **not water barriers** — do not buy expecting dry |

Check with the Kauai County Building Division whether permanently mounted
shutters trigger review, and whether they alter the enclosed-area calculation
for the house.

## 8. Controls caveat

Off-the-shelf automation retracts on rain *and* wind. If the shade's job is rain
exclusion, the correct logic is **deploy on rain detection, retract above a wind
threshold**. Most controllers cannot express this. Crank plus a weather alert
avoids the problem entirely.

## 9. Drainage retrofit — mandatory, independent of blinds

1. Slope deck 1/8–1/4 in/ft to a low edge.
2. Linear trench drain, or 3–4 scuppers through the outer knee wall,
   discharging to a downspout tied to the existing gutter — not to grade below.
3. Fluid-applied or sheet membrane over the deck, turned up 150 mm at walls,
   flashed under the door threshold.
4. 50 mm curb / water dam at the interior door plus a door sweep.
5. Secondary overflow slightly above the primary.
6. Verify floor framing for 100 mm standing water ≈ 21 psf added live load.
7. Extend the windward eave 300–450 mm — intercepts a large share of angled
   rain for a fraction of the cost of any shade.

## 10. Materials and mold/mildew specification

Failure occurs in hardware and detailing, not the fabric:

- 6063-T6 aluminum, powder coat over chromate conversion (salt + vog).
- 316 stainless throughout. No zinc, no galvanized, no mild steel.
- No wood headbox; aluminum or stainless blocking only.
- Cassette end caps with weep slots; brush seal that does not trap water.
- Open-bottom side channels with drain slots.
- Never store the shade rolled up wet — damp fabric in a closed cassette is the
  classic mildew site.
- Twice-yearly wash with dilute bleach or quaternary ammonia, then a
  fresh-water rinse for salt.

## 11. Installation notes

- **Side channels must be plumb within ~2 mm over the 2.0 m drop** or the zip
  binds and tears at the weld. Check and shim the jambs before the shades
  arrive.
- A 3 x 2 m unit is ~15–20 kg — one person with a helper and scaffold is
  realistic.
- Complete blocking and jamb plumb-up **before** ordering.

## 12. Sequence

1. Field-measure all three openings; confirm knee wall and ceiling heights.
2. Order fabric samples; test for moiré and view.
3. Drainage retrofit and windward eave extension.
4. Blocking and jamb plumb-up.
5. Order and install zip shades.
6. Decide hurricane layer (now or deferred).
7. Establish maintenance schedule.
